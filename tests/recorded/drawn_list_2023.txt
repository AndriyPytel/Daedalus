The line work each view is drawn with, taken out of the file and converted to millimetres of the part. Coordinates are millimetres inside the view itself: x to the right of the view's left edge, y *up* from its bottom edge. This is the draughtsman's own geometry and not a reading of a picture, so a distance nobody printed is measured here rather than assumed, and a value measured off it is `derived`, never `not_defined`. Each entity says what it could be proved to be, and most of a sheet is line work nothing can prove anything about:
  `contour` — traced off the picture the view was placed as, and the only thing here that is the part itself.
  `dimension` — a line a printed number was measured against.
  `leader` — followed from a callout to the feature it is of.
  `centre` — drawn dashed, which marks an axis or a plane of symmetry.
  unmarked — an extension line, a hatch stroke or an edge, and nothing here says which. An extension line is drawn from the very face its dimension measures, so it is worth reading and it is not an outline.
An entity marked `traced` is not the draughtsman's: it is measured off the picture, to the inside of the line that draws it, so it reads about a millimetre under the drawn size. Use it to check a number, never to supply one.
What each view is round in, which is the one thing the drawing says outright about direction: a feature is round in the view its axis points at and in no other, so two of them round in two views cannot have been drilled the same way. Say which view each feature is round in, in `round_in`.
