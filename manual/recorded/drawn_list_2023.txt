The line work each view is drawn with, taken out of the file and converted to millimetres of the part. Coordinates are millimetres inside the view itself: x to the right of the view's left edge, y *up* from its bottom edge. This is the draughtsman's own geometry and not a reading of a picture, so a distance nobody printed is measured here rather than assumed, and a value measured off it is `derived`, never `not_defined`. Every entity here says what it was proved to be, and one that could be proved nothing about is not here at all:
  `contour` — traced off the picture the view was placed as, and the only thing here that is the part itself.
  `dimension` — a line a printed number was measured against.
  `leader` — followed from a callout to the feature it is of.
  `centre` — drawn dashed, which marks an axis or a plane of symmetry.
Most of a sheet is an extension line, a hatch stroke or an edge with nothing to say which of the three it is, and that is what each view says it left out. Read out beside the rest, a 40 mm extension line is a 40 mm edge.
An entity marked `traced` is not the draughtsman's: it is measured off the picture, to the inside of the line that draws it, so it reads about a millimetre under the drawn size. Use it to check a number, never to supply one. One marked `read` is that same measurement carrying the size the sheet prints against it, which a callout's leader reached and agreed with.
What each view is round in, which is the one thing the drawing says outright about direction: a feature is round in the view its axis points at and in no other, so two of them round in two views cannot have been drilled the same way. Say which view each feature is round in, in `round_in`.
